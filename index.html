Module 01

Introduction to Liposomes

What are liposomes, where did they come from, and why do they matter in modern pharmacy?

🔬

Definition

Liposomes are microscopic spherical vesicles composed of one or more phospholipid bilayers enclosing an aqueous core. They are the most widely studied drug delivery systems in nanomedicine.

الليبوسومات هي حويصلات كروية مجهرية تتكون من طبقة أو أكثر من الفوسفوليبيدات وتحيط بقلب مائي.

🧫

Basic Concept

Phospholipids are amphiphilic molecules — a water-loving head and water-repelling tails. In water, they spontaneously self-assemble into bilayers, just like the membranes of our own cells.

الفوسفوليبيدات جزيئات مزدوجة المحبة: رأس محب للماء وذيل كاره للماء، فتتجمع تلقائيًا في الماء على شكل طبقات.

🎯

Why Liposomes?

Liposomes improve drug solubility, protect the drug from degradation, prolong circulation time, reduce toxicity, and can deliver the drug preferentially to target tissues with controlled release.

تحسّن الليبوسومات ذوبانية الدواء، تحميه من التكسير، تطيل بقاءه في الدم، وتقلل السمية.

💊

Two Types of Drugs

A liposome can carry both water-soluble drugs (in the aqueous core) and fat-soluble drugs (inside the lipid bilayer) — a unique advantage over most carriers.

يمكن لليبوسوم حمل أدوية محبة للماء وأدوية محبة للدهون في نفس الوقت.

History & Development

1965
Discovery

Alec Bangham first describes phospholipid vesicles — liposomes are born.

1970s
First Drug Delivery

Liposomes are explored as carriers for drugs, enzymes and vaccines.

1980s
Stealth Liposomes

PEGylation ("stealth" coating) prolongs circulation by avoiding the immune system.

1995
Doxil® Approved

The first FDA-approved liposomal drug — liposomal doxorubicin.

Today
Smart Liposomes

Targeted, triggered-release and theranostic liposomes in clinical trials.

Liposomes vs Conventional Drug Delivery

FeatureConventional (Free Drug)Liposomal Delivery
DistributionThroughout the whole bodyPreferentially to target tissues
Half-lifeShort — rapid clearanceProlonged circulation
ToxicityHigh — affects healthy organsReduced — drug is shielded
SolubilityPoor for hydrophobic drugsCarries both hydrophilic & lipophilic drugs
ReleaseImmediate burstSustained / controlled release

Module 05

Drug Loading Simulator

Choose a drug type and watch where it is loaded inside the liposome.

Water-soluble drugs dissolve in the aqueous core.
Aqueous Core Lipid Bilayer Drug Molecules

💧 Hydrophilic Drugs

Loaded in the aqueous core. Examples: doxorubicin HCl, cisplatin, methotrexate.

🫧 Lipophilic Drugs

Loaded inside the lipid bilayer, among the fatty tails. Examples: paclitaxel, amphotericin B, curcumin.

📦 Passive Loading

Drug is present during vesicle formation. Simple, but encapsulation efficiency is often low for hydrophilic drugs.

🚀 Active (Remote) Loading

Drug is loaded after formation, driven by a pH or ion gradient (e.g. ammonium sulfate gradient for doxorubicin). Encapsulation efficiency can reach ~98%.

🧮 Encapsulation Efficiency

EE% = (Encapsulated drug ÷ Total drug) × 100

EE = 85%
